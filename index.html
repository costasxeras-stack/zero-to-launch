Our Money: one budget for two people, without giving up privacy.

The full product journey for one idea, in six stages: what I learned, what I decided, what I built and how I would launch it. Every stage marks what is confirmed, what is a proxy and what is still a hypothesis. Stage 4 is a working prototype you can use.

1 · Discover

Where the idea comes from and what I know so far.

  • BuiltI built Spend Lens for one person's spending. Its next steps already pointed to "shared budgets for two people from two exports".
  • ProxyIn 45 App Store reviews of five money apps (Listening Tour), 27 mention a partner. Splitting and "who owes whom" came with the lowest average rating among common themes (2.93 stars). Bank connection problems came up in 18 reviews.
  • ProxyPrivacy between partners came up in only 2 reviews. Absence in reviews is not evidence of absence, so it goes on the interview list.
  • PendingFive interviews with couples who share some money and keep some separate. The guide is ready in Listening Tour. Results: [to add].

2 · Define

Who

Couples who live together, share some costs (rent, groceries, holidays) and keep some money personal. Each has their own bank. HypothesisThis "partly shared" group is underserved: joint-account apps assume all money is shared, and split apps assume roommates.

Job to be done

"When we pay for shared things from two accounts, help us see what we spent together and whether we are each paying our fair share, so we do not have to keep score or argue about it."

Problem statement

Partly-sharing couples have no single view of shared spending across two banks. They keep score in their heads, a spreadsheet or a split app built for friends, which turns a partner into a debtor. Success is a shared view both trust, a fair-share rule they agree on, and no need to show personal spending.

Principles

  1. Shared is opt-in, per payment. Personal stays personal.
  2. Fair is agreed, not assumed: 50/50 or by income, chosen together.
  3. No bank login. Data stays on devices.

3 · Ideate

Three ways to solve it. I chose the one that tests the riskiest assumption cheapest.

A · Joint account view

Connect both banks, show everything.
Against: needs bank access, breaks principle 3, and shows personal spending.

B · Split app for couples

Log who paid, settle up.
Against: the debt frame is the complaint in the reviews. Already well served.

C · Shared budget with a fair-share rule Chosen

Mark payments as shared, set a budget together, see each person's share against an agreed rule. Personal spending shows as a total only, or not at all.

Riskiest assumptions

  1. Couples will mark payments as shared, payment by payment, if it is one tap.
  2. A fair-share rule reduces arguments more than a running debt.
  3. Partners want to hide personal spending, not only see everything.

6 · Launch

Launch as a Spend Lens feature first

Not a new app. Spend Lens already imports bank exports privately, so "Our Money" becomes a shared space on top of it. That reuses the import and category work, and the roadmap in Now Next Later already sequences it: match transfers and split payments first, shared budgets next.

Rollout

  1. Private beta with the [5] test couples and their friends.
  2. Open beta behind an invite: one partner invites the other, which is the growth loop.
  3. Public launch once the second-month return rate passes [target].

Risks

  • Two-device sync without a server is hard. Mitigation: encrypted export and import between partners first, sync later.
  • Money is emotional. Copy must never blame. "Bartek owes Ala" becomes "Bartek's share is 120 PLN below the rule".
  • Interviews may show couples want full transparency. Then privacy becomes a setting, not the pitch.
